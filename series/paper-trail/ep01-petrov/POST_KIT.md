## 2 · Description (YouTube Shorts)
> Just after midnight on 26 September 1983, a Soviet early-warning satellite reported that the United States had launched five nuclear missiles. The duty officer, Lt. Col. Stanislav Petrov, had to decide whether to pass the alarm up the chain of command. He reported it as a false alarm, and he was right. The "missiles" were sunlight reflecting off high clouds.
>
> He received no medal and was reprimanded for incomplete paperwork. The story stayed secret until 1998.

## 7 · Accuracy notes (keep for comment replies)
- Petrov didn't "refuse to launch"; he couldn't launch anything. He judged the alarm false and reported it as such.
- The cause was sunlight on high-altitude clouds plus the geometry of the Molniya-orbit satellite.
- He died on 19 May 2017. It was not widely reported until September 2017.
- The "survived by… the entire human race" line is Encyclopaedia Britannica's. The VO calls it "one obituary", a loose paraphrase. If challenged, reply with the Britannica source.
