## Fact sheet (every line sourced)
- 26 Sept 1983, shortly after midnight, Serpukhov-15 bunker near Moscow; Oko system reported 1 ICBM, then up to 4 more (5 total): Wikipedia "1983 Soviet nuclear false alarm incident"; LA Times obituary (2017); The Atlantic (2013).
- Display word "LAUNCH" + siren: Petrov to AP, quoted in LA Times obituary 2017.
- KAL 007 shot down 1 Sept 1983, 269 killed: Wikipedia (KAL 007 / incident background); Wired "Day in Tech" 2007 ("barely three weeks" before).
- Petrov, 44, lieutenant colonel, Soviet Air Defence Forces: LA Times; Wikipedia.
- "I had a funny feeling in my gut… When people start a war, they don't start it with only five missiles." — The Washington Post, 1999 (via Miami Herald 2022, The Atlantic 2013).
- Reported it as a false alarm / malfunction; waited; "Twenty-three minutes later I realised that nothing had happened" — BBC 2013 interview (via migflug / National Security Journal).
- Cause: sunlight on high-altitude clouds + Molniya-orbit satellite geometry; later fixed by cross-checking a geostationary satellite: Wikipedia, citing Forden/Podvig/Postol.
- Leaders "given only a couple of minutes to decide" — Bruce G. Blair (Wikipedia). Counterpoint noted: Pavel Podvig — at least three decision layers above Petrov; hence narration says "might have".
- No reward; reprimanded for not filling in the war diary: Wikipedia; LA Times.
- Public only in 1998 (Gen. Yuri Votintsev's memoirs) → 15 years: Wikipedia.
- "My late wife for 10 years knew nothing about it. 'So what did you do?' she asked me. 'Nothing. I did nothing.'" — Petrov, interview for *The Man Who Saved the World* (Wikipedia; axbom.com).
- Died 19 May 2017 (pneumonia, Fryazino); not widely reported until September: Wikipedia.
- "He is survived by his two children, two grandchildren, and the entire human race." — Encyclopaedia Britannica (via Wikipedia).
- Memorial stone, Warsaw — photo by Brandmeister (Togrul Safarov), Wikimedia Commons, **Public Domain**.
